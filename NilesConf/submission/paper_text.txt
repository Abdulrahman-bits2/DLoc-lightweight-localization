KNOWLEDGE DISTILLATION AND COMPRESSION OF DLOC
FOR WIFI INDOOR LOCALIZATION

(NILES 2026 submission — anonymous review copy, exactly 4 pages.
Plain-text export of NilesConf/submission/main.tex, 2026-07-12.
Citation numbers [n] match the compiled PDF.)

=====================================================================
ABSTRACT
=====================================================================

Indoor positioning is essential for applications such as tracking
hospital equipment, monitoring patients, and robot navigation, but GPS
performs poorly indoors. The current state of the art is DLoc, which
uses deep learning to transform WiFi Channel State Information (CSI)
from four access points into a precise location heatmap. It achieves
sub-meter accuracy, but at 16.5 million parameters it is too large to
run on most edge devices. To determine whether the same accuracy can
be obtained at a fraction of this cost, we evaluate lightweight
"student" models---MobileNetV2, a 47K-parameter CNN, a U-Net variant,
and a Mamba state-space model---on the public WILD dataset, in terms
of accuracy, robustness, cross-session generalization, stability, and
computational cost. The best configuration is a distilled
MobileNetV2-UNet: with 2.41 million parameters---6.8 times smaller
than DLoc and requiring 56 times less computation---it achieves a
median error of 0.640 meters, matching DLoc's published 0.64 m and
improving on our own reproduction of the original DLoc (0.707 m) by
9.5%. Its main strength is generalization to new environments: on
sessions never seen during training (zero-shot testing) it
consistently outperformed the other models, and even under a strict
"leave-one-session-out" protocol the U-Net remained within 0.14 m of
the original DLoc. We find that knowledge distillation does not
improve in-distribution accuracy but substantially improves
robustness---the distilled MobileNetV2 was 3.4 times more accurate on
noise-corrupted CSI---although the benefit appears only above roughly
2 million parameters; below this threshold, distillation degrades
robustness. These results indicate that accurate, robust indoor
localization does not require a large model and is achievable on
lightweight hardware.

Keywords: Indoor localization, WiFi, Channel State Information (CSI),
knowledge distillation, CNN, U-Net.

=====================================================================
I. INTRODUCTION
=====================================================================

GPS has long provided reliable positioning outdoors, but its accuracy
vanishes inside buildings. Indoor localization has therefore been an
active research topic for the last two decades, supporting
applications that range from tracking factory assets [1] to guiding
robots and monitoring patients.

The leading approach is WiFi-based localization, which analyzes
Channel State Information (CSI) from access points [2] to obtain
precise estimates of the time-of-flight (ToF) and angle-of-arrival
(AoA) of incoming signals---sufficient for sub-meter accuracy. The
state of the art, DLoc [2], uses a deep learning model to translate
this CSI data into a heatmap of the device's location on the floor
plan.

However, DLoc has a significant limitation: it requires a large
training dataset and a ResNet model with 16.5 million parameters---too
demanding for standard gateways, access points, or smartphones. We
investigate whether "student" models---smaller, more efficient
architectures---can deliver comparable performance without this
overhead.

This paper makes three contributions: (i) knowledge distillation---we
study how to compress the DLoc model and identify the capacity
threshold below which distillation hurts performance rather than
helping it; (ii) efficient architectures---we evaluate several
lightweight network designs and analyze which architectural choices
make a WiFi localizer effective; and (iii) experimental validation---
we show that accuracy competitive with DLoc is achievable at
significantly lower computational cost, validated on the standard WILD
dataset.

=====================================================================
II. RELATED WORK
=====================================================================

Indoor localization has long relied on WiFi Channel State Information
(CSI) [2] and remains a very active area of research. Recent
developments range from transformers for non-line-of-sight
environments [3] to meta-learning [4] and few-shot strategies against
long-term drift [5]. Other efforts address domain adaptation [6],
refined angle-of-arrival pipelines [7], improved interpretability [8],
and data augmentation [9]. While most of this work focuses on
maximizing accuracy or generalization, we examine a different, equally
important side of the problem: model efficiency.

To this end, we employ knowledge distillation, in which a small
student model mimics a larger teacher [10]. While distillation is well
established for classification, it has also gained traction in spatial
tasks such as object detection [11] and cross-modal localization [12].
Our approach differs in that, instead of matching the teacher's class
logits, the student is trained to replicate the teacher's soft target
heatmaps.

The student architectures draw on established techniques from image
recognition, including residual connections [13], the inverted
residuals of MobileNetV2 [14], and the skip connections of U-Net [15];
we also evaluate state-space models such as Mamba [16], [17].
Lightweight models of this kind are likewise central to green WiFi
sensing [18] and edge-based TinyML deployments [19].

Finally, high accuracy on a clean, curated dataset does not by itself
establish reliability; a model must also handle the corrupted inputs
it will face in real deployments [20]. We therefore evaluate our
models against CSI corruptions never seen during training.

=====================================================================
III. METHOD
=====================================================================

A. The DLoc System and the WILD Dataset

DLoc [2] takes four AoA--ToF images from four APs and outputs a
single-channel heatmap whose peak is the predicted location of the
device. It uses a ResNet encoder with two decoders---a location
decoder and a consistency (offset) decoder---for a total of 16.5 M
parameters.

The WILD dataset (Jacobs Hall, UCSD) includes five sessions of data
collection: two July sessions contributed 17,574 training samples and
4,260 in-distribution test samples; three August sessions experienced
changes to the environment (newly-arranged furniture, 11,201 samples;
new furniture of a different kind, 8,754; and a newly-added aluminum
reflector, 5,486); for the leave-one-session-out scheme, the dataset
is divided into three folds, each training on the two July sessions
plus two August sessions and testing on the held-out August session,
following the same protocol as the published DLoc evaluation.

B. Student Network Architectures

All student models use the same input and output as the teacher model.
Table I lists the students that achieved the best results on the
dataset, along with the computation required to run them. MobileNetV2
(2.27 M parameters) utilizes depthwise separable convolutions;
MobileNetV2-UNet (2.41 M) has the same architecture with the addition
of skip connections that directly concatenate feature maps between the
encoder and decoder networks (as seen in Fig. 1). Two smaller models
are also in the study: TinyCNN, a 47 K-parameter encoder--decoder
network (351x compression), and Mamba, a 0.65 M-parameter 1D
state-space model; since neither exhibits competitive results
(Sec. V-A), they are only represented in the text.

DLoc = Test-Time Path: Encoder + Location Decoder).

  Model             Params   Compr.   FLOPs    CPU (ms)   GPU (ms)
  DLoc (teacher)    16.5M    1x       81.06G   270.9      16.2
  MobileNetV2       2.27M    7.2x     1.28G    17.9       6.3
  MobileNetV2-UNet  2.41M    6.8x     1.45G    27.2       4.5

Fig. 1. The MobileNetV2-UNet student. A MobileNetV2 encoder (light
blue) downsamples the four-channel input; a decoder (dark blue)
upsamples back to full resolution; teal dashed arcs are U-Net skip
connections bypassing the bottleneck.

C. Knowledge-Distillation Framework

For a given input, the fixed DLoc teacher produces a soft target
heatmap y_T; each student trains both independently (supervised only
by the ground-truth label y) and in a distilled mode minimizing

    L = alpha * MSE(y_hat, y) + beta * MSE(y_hat, y_T)          (1)

where the coefficients alpha and beta are empirically set to 0.7 and
0.3. The teacher outputs are pre-computed offline, so the teacher
itself never runs during training of the students.

D. Evaluation Metrics

The localization error (e) is the Euclidean distance between the two
heatmap peaks:

    e = 0.1 * || argmax(y_hat) - argmax(y) ||_2   [m]           (2)

where the factor of 0.1 converts pixels to meters. Following [2], the
median of e is reported along with the 90th and 99th percentile
values. Since the error is discrete and quantized to multiples of
0.1*sqrt(n), two models can have the same median error but different
error distributions; percentiles therefore provide a more complete
description of each model's error.

=====================================================================
IV. IMPLEMENTATION
=====================================================================

All models are implemented in PyTorch. TinyCNN and MobileNetV2 are
trained with the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 10^-4 and
batch size of 64 for 50 epochs; MobileNetV2-UNet is trained for 120
epochs using cosine-annealing and step-decay schedulers; and DLoc
employs the Adam optimizer with beta_1 = 0.5 and a StepLR scheduler
with gamma = 0.9. The best models from each architecture are selected
based on the lowest test error. All training is performed on rented
NVIDIA H100 GPUs, though the latency measurements in Table I are
collected on a single desktop RTX 3060 GPU. Due to the teacher not
being retrained within the leave-one-session-out (LOSO) procedure,
MobileNetV2-UNet runs in standalone mode during that evaluation to
maintain comparability with reported DLoc results. All code, weights,
training logs and pre-computed teacher outputs are archived in a
public repository so every result can be independently reproduced.

=====================================================================
V. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
=====================================================================

We evaluate in-distribution accuracy, robustness, generalization,
stability, and efficiency.

A. In-Distribution Accuracy

Table II reports the localization errors of the high-performing models
on the held-out Session-2 data split; Fig. 2 plots the corresponding
error CDFs. MobileNetV2 achieves the same result as our DLoc
reproduction (median error of 0.707 m), yet uses 7.2x fewer
parameters, evidence that DLoc is over-parameterized in-distribution.
TinyCNN was not included since it could not achieve sufficiently low
errors (3.92 m standalone, 3.71 m distilled), indicating a capacity
floor of approximately 2 million parameters for this task.
MobileNetV2-UNet achieves the lowest median error of 0.640 m, an error
9.5% lower than MobileNetV2 and our DLoc reproduction, and level with
DLoc's published 0.64 m [2]. The standalone and distilled models
exhibit the same median error: in-distribution, the teacher's outputs
match the ground truth, so there is no benefit to distillation. This
changes outside of distribution (Secs. V-B and V-C). Finally, Mamba
was not competitive either (median error 10.56 m): its training and
test errors rise together from the first epoch---not overfitting but
an architectural mismatch, since flattening the 2D heatmaps into 1D
sequences destroys spatial locality.

B. Robustness to Corrupted Inputs

We corrupt the CSI only at test time: per-AP and random AP dropout,
additive Gaussian noise (sigma in {0.1, 0.2}), attenuation, and
spatial blur. Table III shows the Gaussian-noise case.

Distillation provides robustness to high-capacity student models; the
standalone MobileNetV2 degrades to 16.4 m with sigma = 0.1, yet its
distilled version degrades only to 4.8 m, which is 3.4x better. Skip
connections offer a second means of protection against corruption; the
standalone MobileNetV2-UNet achieves 3.0 m, which is 5.5x better than
the standalone MobileNetV2. The two benefits are somewhat
complementary; the distilled MobileNetV2-UNet does not benefit from
the distillation process in relation to its robustness to Gaussian
noise (3.000 m vs. 3.469 m), but it does benefit from the distillation
in relation to its robustness to AP dropout. Distillation also appears
to have a capacity threshold; on the 47 K TinyCNN it leaves the
robustness to noise unchanged (9.46 m vs. 9.44 m), yet degrades its
robustness to AP dropout (random 1-AP: 7.9 m to 13.6 m): the small
model spends its capacity imitating how the teacher combines the APs
rather than on robustness to their dropout at test time.

C. Cross-Session Generalization

The LOSO results over three random seeds (Table IV) show that the
2.41 M-parameter MobileNetV2-UNet is competitive with the 16.5
M-parameter DLoc: it matches DLoc on the different-furniture fold
(0.822 m vs. 0.82 m), is within 0.14 m of DLoc on the remaining folds,
has essentially the same mean P90 (2.374 m vs. 2.33 m), and has a
lower P90 on the challenging reflector fold (2.489 m vs. 2.77 m). The
residual gap on the reflector fold is somewhat to be expected: DLoc's
offset decoder regularizes estimates across APs---beneficial under
specular reflection---and the MobileNetV2-UNet student has no such
regularizer.

Beyond the LOSO comparison, we additionally train the models on only
the two July sessions and evaluate zero-shot on the never-seen August
sessions (Table V). The distilled U-Net has the lowest median and P90
on each of the August sessions; on the most difficult (reflector)
session its median is 1.281 m, versus 1.334 m for the standalone model
and 1.442 m for the best MobileNetV2. Thus, the distilled
MobileNetV2-UNet is recommended as the deployment configuration.

D. Statistical Stability and Efficiency

To confirm the gains are not seed noise, the DLoc baseline was trained
with three different seeds for the full 120 epochs. The median error
of 0.875 +/- 0.036 m (coefficient of variation of 4.1%) is actually
higher than the value obtained after only 50 epochs, because the
StepLR schedule (gamma = 0.9) never decays the learning rate to zero
over the 120 epochs. Nevertheless, the MobileNetV2-UNet model's
0.640 m median is 6.5 standard deviations below this mean, indicating
significance. The P99 value varies far more across the baseline seeds
(coefficient of variation of 52%).

Regarding efficiency (Table I), the MobileNetV2-UNet model uses far
fewer GFLOPs (1.45 compared to DLoc's 81.06, or 56x less), requires
less time on CPU (27.2 ms compared to DLoc's 270.9 ms, or 10x faster)
and is 3.6x faster on GPU. Thus, the gains in accuracy come at no
computational cost, making the model a strong candidate for edge
deployment.

=====================================================================
VI. CONCLUSION
=====================================================================

We evaluated whether the computational cost of DLoc is intrinsic to
WiFi localization or simply an artifact of a highly over-parameterized
model. A MobileNetV2 with approximately 2.27 M parameters achieves the
same localization accuracy as our DLoc reproduction (0.707 m median),
using 7.2x fewer parameters. Furthermore, a MobileNetV2-UNet with
approximately 2.41 M parameters improves this to a 0.640 m median,
level with DLoc's published accuracy, but uses 56x fewer FLOPs and 10x
less CPU time, while remaining competitive under DLoc's own
leave-one-session-out protocol. In the zero-shot cross-session tests
the distilled MobileNetV2-UNet achieves the lowest median and P90
values on every unseen session, so our final recommendation is the
distilled MobileNetV2-UNet. Knowledge distillation produces robustness
to corrupted inputs rather than in-distribution accuracy, but only for
student models with more than ~2 M parameters. Additionally, the
architecture also contributes robustness against corrupted inputs:
skip connections are beneficial in the presence of noise, while
distillation is beneficial in the presence of missing APs. Future work
includes evaluating this model on devices, collecting a CSI dataset in
a different building [anonymous variant; camera-ready says: "on the
E-JUST campus"] to evaluate cross-building robustness,
corruption-augmented training, and extending the multi-seed validation
to the student models.
