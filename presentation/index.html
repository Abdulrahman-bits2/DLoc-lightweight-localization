Introduction

From a WiFi signal to a location

  • Each access point measures Channel State Information (CSI) — the complex channel per subcarrier and antenna.
  • Amplitude hints at distance; phase across antennas gives the angle of arrival (AoA); phase across subcarriers gives the time of flight (ToF).
  • But indoors the signal bounces: multipath & non-line-of-sight (NLOS) break classical geometry.
  • And unsynced clocks add an unknown ToF offset that shifts each AP's estimate by 10–15 m.
The core challengeFour APs each give a shifted, multipath-corrupted estimate that disagree. A model has to reconcile them — that's what learning buys us over geometry.
How do you get location from a WiFi signal? The CSI encodes distance through amplitude, angle through phase across antennas, and travel time through phase across frequencies. The hard part: indoor multipath and non-line-of-sight, plus an unknown clock offset that moves each AP's estimate by up to 15 meters. So the four APs disagree, and a learned model reconciles them where classical geometry fails. (~55 s)

DLoc

One encoder, two decoders

  • The location decoder produces the heatmap we read at inference.
  • The consistency (offset) decoder forces the four APs to agree — its job at training time.
What the consistency decoder learnsremoves the clock offset · resolves multipath (picks the direct path) · sharpens low-bandwidth inputs. One AP can't fix itself; four mutually-consistent APs can.
Architecturally DLoc is an encoder with two decoders. A shared ResNet encoder feeds a location decoder — that's the heatmap we use — and a consistency decoder, which is DLoc's clever bit: it forces the four AP maps to agree, and in doing so it learns to remove the clock offset and resolve multipath. Remember the consistency decoder — it comes back when we discuss the reflector session. (~50 s)

Our method

Two levers: architecture & distillation

  • We train each small student in two modes: standalone (ground truth only) and distilled (also match the DLoc teacher).
  • Distilled loss:
    L = 0.7·MSE(ŷ, y) + 0.3·MSE(ŷ, yT)
  • The teacher is frozen and precomputed — never run during student training.

Our two levers are architecture and knowledge distillation. Distillation trains a small student to match the big teacher's output as well as the ground truth, with a 0.7/0.3 weighting. We precompute the teacher once so it never runs in the training loop. The insight: unlike a classifier, our teacher's heatmap is already soft — probability spread around the location — so distillation acts as spatial label smoothing. As you'll see, that buys robustness, not clean accuracy. (~55 s)

Model 1 / 4

Mamba — a sequence model for the signal

The design

A selective state-space model. It reads the channel data as a 1-D sequence and scans along it — there is no 2-D spatial grid.

Why we tried it

Sequence models are very efficient. The question: can a model that never "sees" the floor as an image still localise? It is our smallest student.

Our first student is Mamba — a selective state-space model. Unlike everything else, it treats the channel data as a one-dimensional sequence and scans along it, with no two-dimensional spatial grid. It's the smallest and most efficient option, and we use it to ask whether a model that never sees the floor as an image can still localise. (~35 s)

Model 3 / 4

MobileNetV2 — an efficient encoder–decoder

The design

Depthwise-separable convolutions and inverted residual blocks form a compact encoder; a decoder expands the bottleneck back into a heatmap.

The catch

No skip connections — every bit of spatial detail must squeeze through the narrow bottleneck. The next model fixes exactly this.

Third student, MobileNetV2. Depthwise-separable convolutions and inverted residual blocks make a very compact encoder, which feeds a decoder that expands the bottleneck back to a heatmap. The catch — and it sets up the next slide — is that there are no skip connections, so all the spatial detail has to pass through that narrow bottleneck. (~40 s)

Model 4 / 4 · the hero

MobileNetV2-UNet — the same encoder, plus skips

The design

The same efficient encoder, plus U-Net skip connections (dashed) that carry high-resolution detail straight to the decoder, bypassing the bottleneck.

In localisation, the spatial detail is the answer — the skips preserve exactly what the bottleneck would throw away. This is our main model.

Dataset

WILD: real measurements with ground truth

  • WILD — the public dataset released with DLoc (UC San Diego).
  • Collected by MapFind, a TurtleBot2 robot carrying LIDAR, an RGB-D camera, and a Quantenna WiFi card, driving at 15 cm/s.
  • Ground-truth position from SLAM (RTAB-Map), median error ≈ 5.7 cm; CSI logged every 50 ms.
  • CSI + SLAM position = one labelled example; ~100k points total.

The space we use

The harder 4-AP Jacobs Hall (1500 sq ft). AP 4 is hidden behind a wall (strong NLOS) and a wall of plasma screens behind AP 3 creates rich multipath.

The data is WILD, released with DLoc. It was collected by a robot called MapFind — a TurtleBot with LIDAR, a camera, and a WiFi card — that drives the space slowly while SLAM gives it ground-truth position to about 5.7 centimeters. So every example is real CSI paired with an accurate label. We use the harder four-AP Jacobs Hall space, where one AP is behind a wall and plasma screens create heavy multipath. (~50 s)

Conclusion

What we found

  • DLoc is over-parameterized — 7.2× compression is lossless in distribution.
  • Architecture beats parameters — U-Net skips give 0.640 m, beating the teacher at 6.8× smaller.
  • Distillation buys robustness, not accuracy — 3.4× under noise — with a ~2M capacity threshold below which it harms.
  • Two robustness mechanisms — skips (noise) and distillation (AP dropout) — complementary, not additive.
  • Competitive cross-session at 6.8× compression and 56× lower compute.
Bottom lineDLoc's cost is not inherent — a model an order of magnitude smaller can meet or beat it, ready for the edge.
To conclude — five findings. One: DLoc is over-parameterized; 7.2x compression is lossless. Two: architecture beats parameters; the U-Net's skips beat the teacher at 6.8x smaller. Three: distillation buys robustness, not accuracy, and only above a ~2-million-parameter threshold. Four: the two robustness mechanisms are complementary. Five: competitive cross-session at 56x lower compute. Bottom line: DLoc's cost is not inherent — an order-of-magnitude-smaller model can match or beat it, ready for the edge. (~55 s)

The metric & a quantization caveat

  • Error = 0.1 × pixel distance between predicted and true peak.
  • We report median, P90, P99, mean, and the full CDF.
Why identical medians appearPixel distances quantize the error to multiples of 0.1·√n. Two models can share a median yet differ in distribution — so we never argue from the median alone; we use the mean, percentiles, and CDF.
Backup: the metric is a scaled pixel distance, so errors are quantized to a lattice — which is why some models report the exact same median. We always back it up with mean, percentiles, and the CDF, shown here from the real per-sample errors.
